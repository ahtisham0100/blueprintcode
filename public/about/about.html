SHEET 01 — WHY BLUEPRINT CODE EXISTS

A problem we had before it was a service we sold.

In 2020 our founder started Phoenix Projectors AV from nothing — home cinema, which is about as far from a simple product page as retail gets. A projector is not a decision anyone makes from a photograph and a spec table.

Customers arrived with the same questions every time. Will this screen suit my room? Will this amplifier drive my speakers? Will that projector throw far enough at four metres? Every one of those was a phone call, a hand-written quote, an evening spent measuring someone else's living room by proxy. The sales that were lost were the ones nobody had time to answer.

Agencies quoted for websites. Good websites, in fairness. None of them would build the thing that actually answered the questions, because that thing isn't a website — it's product logic wearing an interface.

So we built it ourselves. Then we built the next one. Blueprint Code is that capability offered to other businesses with the same wound.

We came at this as a business owner who needed better sales tools, not as an agency inventing a service category from theory.

SHEET 02 — WHAT PP-AV TAUGHT US

Six years of running the business we now build for.

Phoenix Projectors AV has traded since 2020. It sells direct, across eight Amazon marketplaces and on eBay's global network. It deals with factories, freight, returns, VAT, marketplace policy and customers who describe a room in terms no specification sheet uses.

That matters more than it sounds. Most configurator projects fail on commercial rules, not on rendering. Which options genuinely can't be combined. What happens to margin when a customer picks the expensive finish. How a configured item reaches a warehouse in a form somebody can actually pick and pack.

We know because we've got it wrong in our own business and paid for it. One of our tools exists specifically because a customer once received the wrong screen, and we wanted a structural fix rather than a promise to be more careful.

SHEET 04 — HOW THE THREE MEET

Commercial logic first. Always.

Most studios start with the interface and retrofit the rules. That's why so many configurators look impressive and sell nothing.

FIRST

Commercial logic

What can be combined, what it costs, what margin it carries, what the business can actually make and ship. Written down before a pixel is drawn.

THEN

UX

The shortest honest path from a customer's rough intention to a specification they trust. Fewer questions, better questions.

THEN

Engineering

Making it fast, correct and durable — with pricing authority on the server, never in the browser.

In practice these overlap constantly. The order matters because it decides what gets sacrificed when something has to give — and what gives should never be the commercial truth of the thing.
