Fig. 01 The problem

Your own materials page puts it well: “it is always important that a candid assessment of room lighting be a key component of your screen selection process.” That is exactly right. It is also, at present, something you ask the customer to do for themselves.

A buyer who is unsure lands on “not sure which screen material is right for your environment?” and is handed an article, then a data sheet, then a phone number. Across StudioTek, FireHawk, Phantom HALR, GrayHawk, SnoMatte, Tiburon, Silver 3D and the Cima range, each with its own gain, half-gain angle, ambient reflectivity and minimum throw, that is a lot of reading before anyone reaches a confident specification.

Every one of those has a known answer, and Stewart already publishes every number needed to reach it. None of them should require a phone call.

On AVS Forum a customer with a 117-inch maximum width worked through Balón Edge at 123 inches diagonal, WallScreen Deluxe at 116.5 and WallScreen 1.5 at 120.75, weighing the masking borders by hand. He was doing arithmetic your frame pages could have done for him in a second, on a forum rather than on your site, and at the end of it he still had no definitive specification to take forward.

Fig. 03 What we build

A pluggable HTML tool that lives on your website, your dealers' websites, or on a rep's tablet in a customer's living room. It does four things:

  • Guides the customer or rep through the decisions that matter — frame type, room lighting, projector throw, speaker placement, gain, aspect ratio, seating distance — and filters to the Stewart materials that actually fit.
  • Sizes the screen from the seating distance, with cinema and immersion reference ranges based on THX and SMPTE standards.
  • Specifies it into a complete, order-ready configuration with a reference code that maps to your ordering system.
  • Closes — the rep takes a deposit or full payment through the tool, and the configured order arrives with you ready to manufacture.

No platform migration. No lock-in. One embeddable tool, built around your product rules.

Fig. 04 Where it sits

It does not need a new page, and it should not have one. It belongs on the frame pages you already have — WallScreen, Balón, Director’s Choice, CineCurve, VistaScope, Luxus — as a Configure button on the page where the customer has already chosen the frame.

The tool inherits that choice and carries on: material, size, ratio, perforation, mount type, LED rail. What those pages describe in prose becomes a decision the customer can actually make.

Fig. 05 Public side, dealer side

You do not publish pricing, and the public tool does not need to. The same product rules can support both a customer-facing specification journey and an authenticated dealer workflow.

Open to the public, it does the hard part — room, throw, ambient light, acoustic requirement, seating distance — and returns a specified screen with a reference code and no price at all. The customer arrives at their dealer knowing exactly what they want, which is a qualified lead you can route to the nearest one rather than a browser who leaves.

Behind the dealer login, the same specification can carry into a quote or order request using that dealer’s authenticated commercial terms. No pricing needs to appear in the public journey.

Authentication, dealer terms and order workflow sit above the standard build. Worth ten minutes on the call to size properly rather than guessing at it here.

Fig. 06 Why this fits Stewart Filmscreen

You have one of the deepest material ranges in the industry. That depth is your competitive advantage and your sales bottleneck at once — nobody chooses between StudioTek 130 G4, FireHawk G5 and Phantom HALR without expert guidance, and that guidance currently has to come from someone on the end of 310-784-5300.

The numbers that decide it are already published. Phantom HALR Plus: gain 1.3, half-gain angle 27 degrees and minimum throw 1.6 times the image width. Those are rules, and rules are what a tool is for. The same holds for every material in the library, and for the MicroPerf X² and CinemaPerf options on top of them.

It would serve Cima too, where buyers are likely to want a fast, clear route to the right specification. A dealer with this open in a customer's room can:

  • Match the right material in two minutes rather than two days of email
  • Specify any size and ratio instantly
  • Generate a reference code that maps to Stewart's ordering system
  • Take a deposit on the spot, while the customer is still in the room

More than seventy years of optical engineering stays in the materials. This puts it in the hands of the people selling them — and in front of the customer who is never going to ring you.
